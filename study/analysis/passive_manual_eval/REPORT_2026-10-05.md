## 1. Reproducibility within one acquisition (2× MVC, 1-2× AVC)

Script `02_reproducibility.py` → `results/20261005_1530/02_reproducibility/`.

**Method.**
- Every pair of same-label slopes in one folder is compared.
- Reported: within-pair SD on log speed (CV %), Bland-Altman bias and limits of agreement, and the
  pooled within-folder SD.
- Subsets: confidence ≥ 2 (main), = 3, resolved, tilted (the reader moved the slider away from the
  automatic start), and confidence ≥ 1.

**Results (MVC = the two MVCs of one ~1 s recording, one beat apart):**

| subset | pairs (subjects) | within-CV | median abs diff | bias (later/earlier) | 95 % LoA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| conf ≥ 2 | 64 (24) | **22 %** | 18 % | −2 % | −46 … +79 % |
| conf = 3 | 35 (13) | 16 % | 19 % | +2 % | −34 … +60 % |
| conf ≥ 2, resolved | 58 (21) | 16.5 % | 18 % | −1 % | −37 … +56 % |
| conf ≥ 2, tilted | 33 (16) | 22 % | 20 % | −1 % | |
| conf ≥ 1 | 86 (32) | 30 % | 21 % | | |
| AVC conf ≥ 2 | 11 (4) | 18 % | 15 % | −4 % | −42 … +58 % |

- **Same line or not.** MVC pairs on the same (reused) line: within-CV 18.5 % (42 pairs). Pairs with a
  separately drawn line: 26 % (22 pairs), and the second MVC reads 12 % slower. Redrawing the line
  adds variability.
- **Both slopes untilted** (both are the automatic speed): 12 %. This is the automatic fit's own
  beat-to-beat scatter. It is lower because the reader accepted the fit only where it looked right.
- **Two AVCs in one recording are rare.** A ~1 s recording holds one AVC at normal heart rates.
  Only 4 subjects contribute AVC pairs, so the AVC figure is weak.
- **The same beat read twice.** 39 events usable in both readings, 20 subjects; other line, other
  window, other session. Single-reading CV **14.6 %** (clear: 12 %), bias −2 %.
  - Beat-to-beat physiology is therefore about √(22² − 15²) ≈ **16 %**.
  - Half or more of the observed MVC-to-MVC difference is the reading itself.
- **MVC vs AVC in the same acquisition** (92 folders, 31 subjects): AVC/MVC median 1.09 per folder,
  1.22 averaged per subject. Wilcoxon over subjects p = 0.01.
  - AVC (end-systole) is faster, as expected.
  - Part of this comes from fast AVC lower bounds (see 09-29 prelim). It holds in the resolved subset (geo-mean 3.9 vs 3.4 m/s).

Figure: `fig1_within_acquisition_BA.png`.

**Interpretation.**
- One acquisition gives an MVC speed to about ±20 %.
- Clear, resolved windows are better (16 %).
- The reading itself (where the slope is drawn and on which line) is the largest single part, so the
  levers are reading rules and semi-automation (points 6 and 7), not more beats.

## 2. Reproducibility between subsequent acquisitions

Same script. Nested random-effects model (REML; subject → acquisition → beat), on log speed, with a
200× subject-level bootstrap for 95 % CIs. 15 subjects have 5-11 acquisitions; the other 23 add to
the subject and residual terms.

| | n slopes (acq, subj) | geo-mean | subject | **between acquisitions** | beat + reading | ICC 1 beat | ICC acq. mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MVC conf ≥ 2 | 171 (107, 34) | 3.7 m/s | 26 % [9-33] | **23 % [15-33]** | 22 % [15-28] | 0.39 | 0.44 |
| AVC conf ≥ 2 | 109 (98, 34) | 4.3 m/s | 21 % [10-28] | **26 % [20-32]** | 17 % [9-23] | 0.32 | 0.33 |
| MVC conf = 3 | 96 | 3.9 | 31 % | 17 % | 17 % | 0.63 | 0.68 |
| AVC conf = 3 | 73 | 4.4 | 5 % | 24 % | 20 % | 0.03 | 0.03 |
| MVC resolved | 158 | 3.4 | 23 % | 25 % | 17 % | 0.38 | 0.41 |
| AVC resolved | 90 | 3.9 | 22 % | 17 % | 14 % | 0.49 | 0.50 |

(CV %; [bootstrap 95 % CI] for the main set. Table: `variance_components.csv`.)

- **An acquisition adds as much variation as the beat and the reading together.** Total
  repeat-acquisition CV is 33 % (MVC) / 31 % (AVC). The speed of one acquisition is a weak estimate of
  the subject.
- **Subjects do differ** (figure `fig2_between_acquisitions.png`). C8 MVC is consistently 6-8 m/s,
  C9 consistently ~2.3 m/s. With a single beat the ICC is only 0.3-0.4. Clear MVCs reach 0.63.
- **AVC clear-only shows no subject differences** (5 %), and the resolved subset does (22 %). AVC
  "clear" includes many near-vertical lower-bound bands that carry no subject information.
- **Precision of a subject mean** (MVC, conf ≥ 2):

  | | 1 beat | 2 beats | 4 beats |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 acquisition | 33 % | 28 % | 26 % |
  | 2 acquisitions | 23 % | 20 % | 18 % |
  | 3 acquisitions | 19 % | 16 % | 15 % |
  | 6 acquisitions | 13 % | 11 % | 10 % |

  **Averaging over acquisitions pays; more beats per acquisition hardly do.** AVC is similar.
- **Acquisition order:** no trend (MVC −2 % per acquisition, p = 0.26; AVC −1 %, p = 0.61).
- **Does a different line position explain it?** Pairs of acquisitions of one subject were compared:
  |Δ speed| against |Δ line depth|, |Δ lateral position|, |Δ angle|, |Δ length| and |Δ HR|, with a
  within-subject permutation test.
  - Only weak links: Δ angle ρ 0.23 (MVC, p 0.12) and 0.27 (AVC, p 0.09); Δ HR for AVC ρ 0.19 (p 0.016).
  - Depth, lateral position and length: nothing.
  - So the probe or line moving between acquisitions is not the main cause. What is left is
    physiology (breathing phase, loading) and the reading.

**Recommendations.**
- Report a subject's speed as the mean over acquisitions: ≥ 3 PLAX acquisitions, with 1-2 usable
  beats each.
- Weight by confidence or restrict to clear + resolved.
- AVC needs the resolved filter (lower bounds out) before it says anything about subjects.

## 3. M-line placement: how it is done and whether it can be automated

Scripts `03_mline.py`, `03b_mline_sensitivity.py` → `results/20261005_1530/03_mline/`.

**How lines are drawn.**
- 123 general lines, 70 % with 2 points. Length 40.5 mm (IQR 35-46). Mid-depth 59 mm (IQR 55-66).
  Orientation 23° from horizontal (IQR 12-37).
- All lines are drawn on buffer 4 (one line on buffer 3).
- The line sits on the **middle of the wall**: across-wall offset of the wall centre from the line
  is median −0.2 mm, IQR −1.2 … +0.7 mm (point 4). It is not on an endocardial edge.
- Event lines (365): 75 reused automatically, 93 accepted unchanged at the prompt, **197 redrawn**.

**How far does the reader move the line for an event?** Mean distance of the drawn event line to
the general line:

| | n | median distance | median angle | within 1.5 mm & 5° |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MVC (redrawn) | 56 | 1.0 mm | 2.3° | 59 % |
| AK | 18 | 1.3 mm | 2.4° | 56 % |
| **AVC** | 121 | **3.7 mm** | **6.8°** | 12 % |

The AVC line also starts a median **8 mm** further along the septum (r = 0 end) and is 5 mm
longer. That is the basal descent of the heart in systole. The along-line position changes r, not
the speed.

**Reference: the reader's own scatter.** The September general lines (buffer 1, months earlier)
against today's (37 folders): median **1.06 mm, 2.9°**. A prediction this close is as good as a
redraw.

**A. Event lines from the general line** (the `swp.mline.transfer` registration of the buffer-4

| registered with rotation ±20° (AVC) | | | 1.8 | |

| registered + wall snap | 1.7 mm | | | |

**B. General lines.**

| prediction | n | median distance | median angle | ≤ 1 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| September line (same reader) | 37 | 1.06 mm | 2.9° | 43 % |
| previous acquisition's line, as drawn | 84 | 1.32 mm | 3.9° | 39 % |

| reader's line + wall snap | 123 | moved 1.5 mm | | |

Figure: `fig1_line_prediction.png`.

**C. How precise must a line be?** For 24 clear, resolved windows (12 MVC, 12 AVC, one per folder),
the five space-times were recomputed (the worker's own code path) on the drawn line and on perturbed
copies:

| perturbation | slant stack vmed / vg / acc: median (p75) speed change | amplitude |
|---|---|---|
| ±1 mm across | 0-10 % (8-16 %) | ×0.97-1.02 |
| ±2 mm across | 8-15 % (19-30 %) | ×0.95-1.03 |
| ±5° | 0-14 % (15-20 %) | ×0.98-1.01 |

(The 0 % / ~10 % steps are the slowness grid of the slant stack.)
- Within ~1 mm / 5° the speed changes less than the reading noise (15 %); 2 mm starts to add.
- The amplitude hardly changes, so the wave is not confined to a thin band on the wall centre.

**Conclusions and proposal.**
1. **MVC and AK event lines: no prompt needed.** Registration lands within the reader's own
   scatter (0.8-1.0 mm). Extend the MVC reuse (now only ≤ R+50 ms with perp ≤ 1 mm) to all MVC and
   AK windows, and save the *registered* line instead of the identical general line. Show it in the
   review for spot checks.
2. **AVC event lines: pre-load the registered line (with rotation), the reader corrects.** It
   starts at ~1.8-2.2 mm instead of 3.7 mm, so less dragging. Full automation is not supported
   yet: the error is twice the reader's scatter and above the 1 mm tolerance.
3. **General lines, acquisitions 2..n of a subject: pre-load the previous acquisition's line,
   registered onto the new R-peak frame** (ENTER accepts). This is as close as the reader's own redraw.
4. **From scratch (first acquisition): keep drawing by hand.** A simple wall-contrast search is
   unreliable, and even moves correct lines by 1.5 mm. The wall centre is not the brightest-contrast
   line. After the reading, ~500 drawn lines would be enough to train a proper detector
   (e.g. keypoints for the basal and apical end).
5. The registration's own "trusted" flag helps: trusted 1.3 mm vs all 1.5 mm. Keep showing it.

## 4. Septal thickness from these images

Scripts `04_septum.py`, `septum.py` → `results/20261005_1530/04_septum/`.

**Method.**
1. Intensity profiles (dB) are taken along the local normal of the drawn line, ±20 mm, over the
   middle 70 % of the line, averaged over ±2 mm along it.
2. In each profile, the wall centre (within ±6 mm of the line) and the RV- and LV-cavity floors
   (4-16 mm either side) are found.
3. The edges are placed by three rules:
   - **half**: half level, walking out from the centre;
   - **grad**: steepest slope;
   - **outin**: walking in from each cavity floor to floor + max(6 dB, 30 % of the contrast).
4. A frame is "measurable" when ≥ 50 % of positions have ≥ 6 dB contrast on both sides.
5. Measured at end-diastole on buffers 1 / 3 / 4 (the R-peak frames of the general line), on every
   buffer-1 R-peak, on adjacent buffer-1 frames, and at the event frames (AVC ≈ end-systole).

**Measurable at ED:** buffer 3 (focused) **98 %**, buffer 1 (widebeam) 89 %, buffer 4 (diverging) 60 %.
Median contrast: buffer 3 RV/LV 16/21 dB, buffer 1 11/18 dB, buffer 4 7/12 dB.

| edge rule | ED median (b1 / b3 / b4) | b3−b1 (bias ± SD, r) | adjacent frames SD | beat SD | between acq. SD | subject SD | AVC / ED (b3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| half | 6.5 / 7.0 / 5.5 mm | +0.4 ± 1.4, 0.67 | 0.75 | 0.86 | 1.29 | 1.12 | 0.97 (41 % thicker) |
| grad | 8.0 / 8.1 / 5.7 | +0.5 ± 2.2, 0.67 | 1.18 | 1.66 | 1.44 | 1.71 | 1.14 (61 %) |
| outin | 11.7 / 11.1 / 9.0 | 0.0 ± 1.7, 0.73 | 0.80 | 1.27 | 1.15 | 2.10 | 1.14 (70 %) |

(normal adult IVSd ≈ 6-11 mm; normal systolic thickening ≈ 30-50 %)

**What works.**
- Buffer 3 gives a measurable wall in almost every PLAX folder.
- Frame-to-frame precision is 0.75-0.8 mm.
- Buffers 1 and 3 (different beats) agree without bias for "half" and "outin".
- The values are in the physiological range.

**What does not (yet).**
- **The value depends on the edge rule by ~5 mm** (6.5 to 11 mm on the same images). The blood
  pools are not anechoic (clutter, harmonic noise), the wall has internal bright and dark layers,
  and trabeculae, the moderator band and chordae sit close to the LV side. Example profiles:
  `fig2_profiles_b3.png`.
- **Systolic thickening is reproduced only partly:** ×1.14 at best, in 61-70 % of folders, where
  ×1.3-1.5 in ~all is expected. At AVC the wall is thicker and moves more, and the "half" rule even
  reports thinning. This is the main reason not to trust the numbers yet.
- With "half", subject SD (1.1 mm) is smaller than acquisition-to-acquisition SD (1.3 mm): no
  subject information. "outin" separates subjects better (2.1 vs 1.15 mm), but it is also the rule
  most exposed to trabeculae.
- Buffer 4 (what the speeds come from) is the worst image for this.

**Verdict.** Septal thickness from these data is **feasible as a rough number (±1.5-2 mm),
not yet as a validated measurement**. To make it usable:
1. Measure IVSd by hand (calipers, standard PLAX position at the mitral leaflet tips, end-diastole)
   on buffer 3 for ~20 folders. Choose and calibrate the edge rule against that. ~15 min of work;
   this script then gives the rest.
2. Use buffer 3 (or 1) at the R-peak, never buffer 4.
3. Re-check systolic thickening with the chosen rule as the validity test.

**Why it matters for the speeds:** at 50-150 Hz and 3-5 m/s the wavelength is 20-100 mm, much longer
than a ~10 mm wall. The septum then acts as a plate, and the phase speed of the guided (Lamb-type)
wave depends on thickness × frequency. A thickness per subject would be the input for a dispersion
correction, which makes the validation step above worthwhile.

## 6. Slope fitting in the space-time plots

Script `06_slopes.py` → `results/20261005_1530/06_slopes/`.

**Reference sets, and the anchoring problem.**
- The slider starts at the automatic velocity-gauss speed. 37 % of usable slopes were accepted
  untilted (47 % of the clear ones, 48 % on 10-01, 23-34 % later). On those, automatic = hand
  by construction.
- The fair test is therefore the **tilted** slopes, but that set is biased *against* the automatic
  fit (it is where the reader disagreed). Both are reported.
- Errors are also expressed as the **delay error across the whole M-line**, in ms and frames
  (1 frame = 1.08 ms). That is the unit in which a hand slope is precise, and it does not blow up
  for near-vertical bands.

**Current automatic fit vs hand** (slant stack, velocity gauss):

| set | n | median speed error | within 30 % | bias | wrong sign / none | median delay error | within 2 frames |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| conf ≥ 2, tilted, resolved | 180 | 33 % | 47 % | **+18 %** | 14 % | 3.3 ms | 33 % |
| conf = 3, tilted, resolved | 86 | 21 % | 70 % | +17 % | 3.5 % | 2.1 ms | 52 % |
| conf ≥ 2, all | 309 | 17 % | 64 % | 0 % | 10 % | 1.5 ms | 58 % |
| conf = 1 | 47 | 149 % | 23 % | | 45 % | 9.4 ms | 23 % |

Where it fails (tilted, |error| > 30 %, 107/196):
- 61 are too fast, 16 too slow, 30 have the wrong sign, and 14 are railed at 20 m/s.
- Near-vertical bands pull the fit fast.
- The in-phase basal block and the flat fraction are no different between failures and successes,
  so they are not the cause.

**Alternatives** (`estimator_scores.csv`). Main set = conf ≥ 2, tilted, resolved:

| estimator | median error | within 30 % | bias | delay error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **slant stack, velocity median** | 29 % | 52 % | +10 % | **2.8 ms** |
| slant stack, acceleration | 28 % | 53 % | +12 % | 3.0 ms |
| median of the five slant stacks | 29 % | 51 % | +17 % | 3.0 ms |
| **reader's anchor click + automatic tilt (on the anchor view)** | **27 %** | 53 % | **+1 %** | 3.2 ms |
| best one-polarity line, Keijzer | 32 % | 47 % | +6 % | 3.2 ms |
| current (velocity gauss) | 33 % | 47 % | +18 % | 3.3 ms |
| slant stack with flat-band removal | worse | | | |
| arrival-time (cross-correlation) fit | 52 % | 38 % | +24 % | |

- On **clear** tilted windows, the anchor + automatic tilt is best: 16 % median error, 49 % within
  15 %, bias +2 %.
- An estimator chosen leave-one-subject-out reaches 30 % median error. No automatic fit reaches the
  reader's own re-read precision (15 %).
- The hand line **follows the zero crossing of the velocity bands, i.e. the acceleration extremum**.
  This explains:
  - why the hand line scores low on velocity panels and high on acceleration panels;
  - why most clear slopes (108/179) are anchored on the acceleration panel;
  - why a polarity-ridge objective on velocity does not reproduce the hand lines.
- Examples of hand vs automatic lines: `fig1_estimators_vs_hand.png`. Most large %-errors are
  near-vertical bands, where the three lines are almost on top of each other.

**Proposals** (no change made; for after the reading):
1. **Start the slider at the velocity-median (or acceleration) slant-stack speed** instead of
   velocity gauss. That halves the fast bias of the start (+10-12 % vs +18 %), so fewer untilted
   acceptances of a fast start.
2. **Semi-automatic tilt:** after the anchor click, set the tilt to the best line through the
   anchor on the clicked view, and let the reader fine-tune. On clear waves this matches the hand
   tilt best (bias +1-2 %), and it removes the anchoring on a whole-panel fit.
3. **Report speeds with a delay uncertainty** (± frames across the line) rather than a %. Flag
   crossing in < 5 frames as a lower bound in the export (open since 09-29).
4. For a fully automatic speed, fit on acceleration / velocity median, restrict the fit to the
   window without pads, and treat ≥ 6 m/s as "fast, unresolved".

**Proposals.**
1. **Write down the scoring rule** the later sessions converge to. For example: 3 = a clean ridge
   on the acceleration panel that follows a resolved slope (≥ 5 frames); 2 = visible on velocity,
   not clean on acceleration, *or* clean but near-vertical; 1 = guess.
2. **Re-score the 10-01 folders blind** with the session's `--redo slope` (or a quick re-score-only
   pass), or at least the flagged windows above.
3. **Show the new metric as a hint after the score is given** (not before, to avoid anchoring), or
   flag disagreements ≥ 1 for a second look at the end of a session.
4. Keep confidence separate from the "fast, unresolved" flag. The reader currently folds both into
   one score, which is the main disagreement with any amplitude-based metric.

## Caveats

- **Preliminary, one observer, one snapshot while reading.** 15 multi-acquisition subjects carry
  the between-acquisition estimates (wide CIs).
- **The "same beat read twice" pairs differ in more than the reading:** detector, window, line
  and session (09-29 vs 10-01/05). They are an upper bound on pure reading noise for an identical
  line.
- **Septum:** no ground truth; values depend on the edge rule (see 4).
- **Slopes:** the hand slope is the reference, and anchoring to the slider start makes the tilted
  / untilted split necessary. The 2D arrival maps (10-01 prelim) remain the only independent check,
  for clear windows.
- **Quality model:** 17 features on 363 windows from 38 subjects, LOSO-validated. Re-fit after the
  reading before using it for anything automatic.
